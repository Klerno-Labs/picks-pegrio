Oscillating vs. Sonic: The Evidence

A 2014 Cochrane Review analyzing 56 studies found oscillating-rotating brushes removed 11% more plaque and reduced gingivitis 17% more than sonic brushes. Philips disputes this with their own research showing sonic fluid dynamics clean between teeth better. The practical difference for most users is minimal — both massively outperform manual brushing.

Pressure Sensor: The Most Useful Feature Under $50

Over-brushing causes gum recession. Pressure sensors (red light indicator on both Oral-B Pro 1000 and Philips 4100) alert you when you're pressing too hard. This single feature prevents the most common electric toothbrush mistake. Both models at $49 include pressure sensing — cheaper models don't.
