Bare Cast Iron vs. Enameled

Bare cast iron (Lodge) requires seasoning — building a polymerized oil coating through use. Enameled cast iron (Le Creuset) has a vitreous enamel interior that's non-reactive and requires no seasoning. Bare is better for high-heat searing; enameled is better for acidic foods (tomatoes, wine sauces) and easier to clean.

What's in a Good Set

A useful cast iron set includes at minimum: a 10 or 12-inch skillet, a 4-6 quart Dutch oven, and lids. The Lodge 5-piece adds a griddle, lid with handle, and a small skillet. For most home cooks, the skillet and Dutch oven are the workhorses — the rest are nice additions.

Induction Compatibility

All bare cast iron is induction compatible. Enameled cast iron is induction compatible if the manufacturer specifies it — most Le Creuset pieces are. Check the product listing for induction compatibility before purchasing if you have an induction cooktop.
