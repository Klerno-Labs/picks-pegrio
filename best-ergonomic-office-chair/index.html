If you sit for six or more hours a day, your chair is one of the most important purchases you'll make. The wrong chair causes cumulative spinal compression, hip flexor tightening, and lower back fatigue that compounds over months into real musculoskeletal problems. The right ergonomic chair — properly adjusted for your body — removes that load and lets you stay focused without the 2pm back ache that signals your body is done.

What to Look for in an Ergonomic Office Chair

Most buyers focus on features and specs, but the five factors below predict whether a chair actually reduces pain and fatigue for you specifically.

1. Lumbar Support Adjustability (Not Just Presence)

Having lumbar support is not the same as having useful lumbar support. A fixed lumbar bump that hits your lower back at waist level is often worse than no lumbar support at all — it pushes your spine into a curved position that isn't your natural lumbar curve. What you need is adjustable lumbar height (to position the support at your L3-L5 vertebrae, not your waist) and adjustable lumbar depth (to control how far it pushes into your back). The Herman Miller Aeron's PostureFit SL is the best implementation of this concept because it supports two points — sacrum and lumbar — independently.

2. Seat Depth Adjustment

The seat should leave 2-3 finger widths between the front edge of the seat and the back of your knees. If you can't achieve this — because the seat is too deep for your leg length — you'll unconsciously slide forward and lose lumbar support contact. Seat depth adjustment is more important than armrests for most users but is missing from most chairs under $300.

3. Armrest Adjustability (Height AND Width)

Armrests that can't be adjusted inward enough force your elbows out, elevating your shoulders and creating upper trap tension. Armrests too high do the same thing. Correct position: elbows at 90 degrees, shoulders relaxed down, forearms resting on armrests without shoulder elevation. Most people's armrests are set too high.

4. Seat Material for Your Climate

Mesh seats (Aeron, Branch) allow airflow and reduce heat buildup during long sessions. Foam-padded seats feel softer initially but compress over time and retain heat. In hot climates or for users who run warm, mesh is noticeably more comfortable for 6+ hour sessions.

5. Weight Capacity for Your Size

Most ergonomic chairs are rated for 250-300 lbs. The Herman Miller Aeron size C is rated for users up to 350 lbs. If you're near the weight capacity of a chair, the structural integrity decreases faster and the adjustment mechanisms wear out sooner. Always check weight rating for your actual weight, not just height.

How to Set Up Any Ergonomic Chair Correctly

Most ergonomic benefit is lost when chairs are never properly adjusted. Here's the correct sequence:

  1. Seat height first: Sit with feet flat on the floor. Knees should be at 90 degrees. If your feet dangle, use a footrest. If your knees are above your hips, raise the seat.
  2. Lumbar support positioning: Slide the lumbar support up or down until it sits in the curve of your lower back — this is typically at navel height, not at your waist. The support should push your back slightly forward into its natural curve.
  3. Seat depth: Slide the seat forward or back until there are 2-3 finger widths between the seat edge and the back of your knees.
  4. Armrest height: Lower the armrests until your shoulders fully relax. Most people have armrests too high, which elevates the shoulders and causes upper back tension.
  5. Recline tension: Most chairs default to tight recline. Loosen it until leaning back requires minimal effort. Light recline (10-15 degrees) throughout the day reduces spinal disc pressure compared to rigidly upright sitting.
  6. Monitor height: This is the step most people skip. Adjust monitor height until the top of the screen is at or slightly below eye level. Looking down at a screen positioned too low causes the same upper back problems as a bad chair.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a $1,500 Herman Miller Aeron worth it?

For people who sit 6-8 hours per day and have existing back issues, yes. The Aeron's PostureFit SL independently supports sacrum and lumbar, which no chair under $700 replicates correctly. Amortized over its 12-year warranty life, the cost is roughly $125-150 per year — less than a monthly gym membership. If you sit fewer than 4 hours daily, a mid-range chair at $400-600 provides adequate ergonomics for most people.

What chair height setting should I start with?

Set chair height so your feet rest flat on the floor with your knees at 90 degrees. Your thighs should be roughly parallel to the floor. From there, adjust your desk height so your elbows are at 90 degrees when your hands rest on the keyboard. Most people set chair height correctly but fail to adjust their desk, forcing a forward shoulder hunch that causes upper back pain.

What's the best ergonomic chair for lower back pain?

For diagnosed lower back conditions, the Herman Miller Aeron with PostureFit SL is the most clinically supported option. For general lower back fatigue from sitting, any chair with adjustable lumbar support positioned at the correct curve of your spine reduces pain significantly. The Steelcase Leap V2's LiveBack technology adapts to your spinal shape in real time, which many physical therapists recommend specifically for people with chronic lower back issues.

How do I know if my current chair is causing back pain?

Signs your chair is causing back pain: pain that increases throughout the workday and subsides after standing or walking, pain concentrated in the lower back rather than the upper back (upper back pain is usually monitor height), and pain that worsens when you sit for more than 30 minutes. A chair that doesn't support the natural S-curve of your spine forces your lumbar muscles to hold the load, causing fatigue and eventually chronic pain.

Should I buy a used Herman Miller Aeron?

Yes, from a certified remanufacturer. Dealers like Crandall Office Furniture and Madison Seating replace worn components, clean the mesh, and offer 2-year warranties on remanufactured Aerons. You can get a size B Aeron for $400-650 this way — a dramatic savings over new. Avoid individual eBay or Craigslist listings where you can't verify the chair's condition or original size.
