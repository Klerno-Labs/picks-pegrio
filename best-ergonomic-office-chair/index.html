Office chairs: compare fit and configuration before the brand

Sources checked October 10, 2026 · By Picks by Pegrio

Evidence note — October 10, 2026

We checked the current manufacturer sources linked below. We have not tested these products. Features are manufacturer statements; buying considerations are editorial judgment. This revision used AI-assisted research. All external links on this page are direct, unpaid references.

Choose an office chair around the person, desk and tasks it must support. Try the controls and sitting position when possible. A chair described as ergonomic is not automatically a good fit, and a high price does not establish that it prevents pain.

One model name can cover many configurations

Herman Miller’s Aeron specification page lists sizes A, B and C and several arm, back-support and tilt options. Arm choices include fixed, height-adjustable, fully adjustable and no arms. Back-support and tilt features also vary by configuration.

Check the exact size and option list rather than assuming every Aeron includes the same adjustments. A used or refurbished chair needs a separate condition and seller review; a current new-chair specification does not prove which parts or warranty a particular used unit has.

Check the working position

Assess whether you can reach the keyboard and pointer comfortably, whether the armrests clear the desk, and whether the seat works for your legs and chosen foot support. Check the minimum and maximum adjustments that matter to you rather than choosing size B by default.

Try the chair through the tasks you actually do: typing, reading, calls and changes in position. Seat feel, mesh feel and control placement are personal factors. We have not performed a comfort trial or measured pressure, posture or injury risk.

Compare the complete offer

Record the configuration, seller, shipping, assembly, return charges and applicable warranty. If a trial is available, read its conditions. Compare those terms before treating a lower advertised price as better value.

Persistent pain or symptoms need appropriate individual guidance. This guide does not establish that a chair cures a condition or that one model is a clinical benchmark.
