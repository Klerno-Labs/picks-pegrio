Blackout vs. Room-Darkening: The Real Difference

True blackout curtains use a black yarn woven between two layers of fabric or a blackout coating on the back. They block 99-100% of light. Room-darkening curtains block 85-95% — enough to sleep during the day but you'll still see light around the edges. The NICETOWN panels use triple-weave technology rated at 99.9% light blocking.

Thermal Benefits Beyond Darkness

Blackout curtains reduce heat gain through windows by 24-33% in summer (U.S. Department of Energy estimate). In winter, they reduce heat loss. For bedrooms with west-facing or south-facing windows that get afternoon sun, blackout curtains noticeably reduce air conditioning load and room temperature.

Getting True Blackout: Installation Matters

Even perfect blackout fabric leaks light at the edges if the curtain rod extends only to the window frame width. For true blackout, mount the rod 6-12 inches beyond the window frame on each side and hang curtains that extend to the floor with no gap at the bottom. A curtain wider than the window is required for complete coverage.
