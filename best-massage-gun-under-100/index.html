Amplitude: The Number That Matters Most

Amplitude is the stroke length — how far the attachment travels into your muscle. The Theragun Pro has 16mm. The Theragun Relief (budget line) has 12mm. Most $50-80 guns have 10-12mm. For general soreness, 10mm is sufficient. For deep tissue work on dense muscles (quads, glutes), 14mm+ makes a noticeable difference.

Stall Force for Different Bodies

Stall force is how much pressure the gun can handle before the motor stalls. Budget guns stall at 20-30 lbs. The Renpho R3 handles 35 lbs — enough for casual use on most users. Professional-level guns at $250+ handle 60 lbs, which matters for athletes and bodyworkers. For personal recovery, 30-40 lbs is sufficient.

Noise: A Real Quality-of-Life Factor

The cheapest guns run at 60-70 dB — about as loud as a conversation. Mid-range ($60-100) runs 45-55 dB. Theragun's QuietForce technology runs at 40 dB or below. If you're using it while watching TV or near a partner who's sleeping, the quieter models are worth the extra cost.
