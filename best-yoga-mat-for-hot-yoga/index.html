Yoga mats for hot yoga: surface, care and carrying weight

Sources checked October 10, 2026 · By Picks by Pegrio

For hot yoga, choose a mat by the surface you want to use, its care requirements and whether you are willing to add a towel. “Rubber,” “PVC” and “grippy” are not complete descriptions of how a finished mat handles moisture. No mat specification guarantees that you will never slip.

The Manduka PRO page explains that its closed-cell surface does not absorb moisture and may feel slick with sweat. It recommends an Equa or Yogitoes mat towel for heated practice. Choose this option with the towel’s cost, care and carrying bulk in mind; it is not our recommendation for guaranteed towel-free grip.

lululemon’s current The Mat 5mm page describes a moisture-absorbing top layer and reversible construction. Its latex warning is explicit: people with rubber or latex allergies should avoid contact. This is the named current version, not every older Reversible Mat, Big Mat or other lululemon model.

The Gaiam Dry-Grip page describes a moisture-wicking topcoat on PVC. That is a useful counterexample to the claim that all PVC mats behave alike. Its listed weight is lower than the two standard-size examples above, but it is also shorter and narrower. We have not tested the brand’s grip claims.

Choose a surface and a cleaning routine together

Consider whether you want a separate washable towel or prefer direct contact with the mat. Look at the exact surface construction and care instructions, not a universal “open-cell rubber versus closed-cell PVC” rule. The product’s suitability also depends on whether you will actually have space and time to dry it between classes.

Manduka’s care guide distinguishes cleaning by mat type. lululemon says to let The Mat dry before rolling and store it out of direct sunlight. Gaiam calls for spot cleaning and drying flat, and warns against prolonged sunlight. Do not assume that soaking, machine washing or a studio’s cleaning spray is appropriate for every mat.

Check dimensions before choosing by weight

Mark out the stated length and width at home, then compare them with your current mat or the space you use in class. A shorter mat may be easier to carry but may not give you the coverage you want. Extra-long versions can have different weights and dimensions; the table does not apply to those variants.

If you already own a suitable mat, first review its cleaning instructions and the manufacturer’s guidance on towels. A different towel or care routine may address the purchasing problem without replacing the mat. We have not assessed your mat’s condition or compared these products in a heated studio.

Before ordering

Confirm the exact version, materials, color availability and return conditions for a used mat. Include any towel and carrying accessory in the total cost. Choose on the documented tradeoffs that matter to you; claims such as “best grip” or “joint protection” are not independent measurements in this guide.
