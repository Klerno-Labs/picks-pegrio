What to Look for in a Standing Desk Under $300

Motor Type: Single vs. Dual

Dual motors lift each leg independently, distributing weight evenly and reducing wobble at standing height. Single motors use a crossbar or belt to drive both legs from one mechanism — functional but slower and less stable. For light setups (monitor, laptop, keyboard), single motor is fine. For heavy workstations (dual monitors on arms, desktop PC, speakers), dual motors like the Flexispot E7 are worth the $50-80 premium.

Height Range for Your Body

The desk must reach your standing elbow height. Measure by standing with elbows at 90 degrees and measuring from floor to forearm. Most adults 5'4" to 6'2" need 38-46 inches. The Flexispot E7's 22.8-48.4 range covers nearly everyone. The SHW's 28-inch minimum is too high for shorter users sitting in low chairs — check minimum height against your sitting position.

Stability and Leg Design

3-stage telescoping legs (three overlapping sections) are more stable than 2-stage because the overlap distributes lateral force better. Wobble at standing height comes from leg flex, not motor quality. If you type vigorously or use a mechanical keyboard, 3-stage legs (Flexispot E7) noticeably reduce screen shake versus 2-stage alternatives.

Desktop Size and Material

48x24 inches is the minimum functional size for a workstation. 55x24 accommodates dual monitors on arms. 60x30 is ideal but rare under $300. Material matters: bamboo ($30 extra on Flexispot) is harder and more scratch-resistant than particle board. Solid wood tops aren't available under $300 — particle board with melamine veneer is standard.

Cable Management

Cable management is the most overlooked standing desk feature. When the desk moves up and down, cables need slack or they pull. Look for desks with cable trays, grommet holes, and included cable ties. The FEZIBO includes all three; most others require aftermarket solutions ($10-15).

How to Use a Standing Desk Correctly

Buying the desk is step one. Using it correctly is where most people fail. The key is alternating, not standing all day. Start with 30 minutes standing per hour and adjust based on comfort. Position your monitor at eye level (top of screen at or slightly below eye level) when standing — this often requires a monitor arm ($25-50) since desk-height monitors are too low when you're standing. Wear supportive shoes on hard floors; anti-fatigue mats ($20-35) reduce foot and leg fatigue significantly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are standing desks under $300 stable?

Most are stable enough for daily work. Dual-motor desks (Flexispot E7) distribute weight across both legs, reducing wobble at standing height. Single-motor desks handle 100-150 lbs without issues. Wobble increases with heavy monitors — if you have dual 27-inch monitors on arms, expect some movement at full standing height. Look for 3-stage telescoping legs for better stability.

How high should a standing desk go?

Standing desk height should position your elbows at 90 degrees when typing. For most adults 5'4" to 6'2", that's 38-46 inches. The Flexispot E7 ranges from 22.8 to 48.4 inches, covering users from 4'9" to 6'7". Measure your ideal standing elbow height before buying.

Do I need a dual motor standing desk?

Dual motors provide smoother transitions, better stability, and longer motor life. Single-motor desks work fine for loads under 150 lbs but may struggle with dual monitors, a desktop PC, and heavy peripherals. The $50-80 premium for dual motors is worth it for full workstations.

Can I assemble a standing desk by myself?

Yes, but assembly is easier with two people. The heaviest component is the desktop (30-50 lbs). Most desks ship flat-pack with Allen wrench assembly in 45-90 minutes. The frame assembles with 8-12 bolts; attaching the desktop uses wood screws or brackets.

What size standing desk do I need?

48x24 inches fits one monitor, keyboard, and mouse. 55x24 inches accommodates ultrawide or dual monitors on arms. 60x30 inches suits multi-monitor workstations. Allow at least 24 inches of clearance behind the desk for your chair. For small apartments, 48-inch is the minimum functional width.
