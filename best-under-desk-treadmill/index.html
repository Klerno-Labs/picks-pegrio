Under-desk treadmills: measure the workspace before buying

Sources checked October 10, 2026 · By Picks by Pegrio

An under-desk treadmill needs to fit both your workspace and your working habits. Start with the usable belt area, desk clearance, controls and return terms. A compact folded size or an advertised motor rating does not establish comfortable typing, quiet calls or reliable daily operation in your home.

Measure the full setup

  • Working position: Account for the deck’s step-up height when checking the desk and monitor range. Overall machine height and walking-surface height are different measurements.
  • Floor space: Compare the complete footprint, required clearances in the manual and access to controls. Leave room for safe access and storage as directed by the manufacturer.
  • Moving it: Check machine weight and the route to its storage position. Folding does not make a heavy unit easy for everyone to lift.
  • Controls: Establish how to stop the belt and whether a remote or other required controller is included. Read the actual manual before use.

Noise and maintenance need local assessment

We have not measured these machines at a standardized speed, load, microphone distance or floor type. The previous 40 dB and 50–55 dB comparison has been removed. Footfalls and vibration can matter as much as a motor specification. If quiet operation is essential, ask about a demonstration and review return freight and restocking terms before ordering.

Check the model’s maintenance schedule, electrical requirements, replacement parts and warranty conditions. Use it only in the operating modes permitted by its manual. This guide does not promise that walking while working improves cognition or offsets health risks.

Check the exact model against recall information

Use the US Consumer Product Safety Commission’s recall search with the brand, model and identifying details, and check manufacturer notices before buying, including secondhand units. Our bounded review did not establish a recall affecting the named A1 Pro or TR1200-DT3. It also cannot certify any treadmill as recall-free or safe. A similar brand name or a recall of a different treadmill is not evidence about your exact unit.
