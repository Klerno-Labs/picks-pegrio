Ear Tip vs. Ear Hook Fit Systems

Standard silicone ear tips (AirPods, Jabra style) work well for most people at walking and easy run paces. At faster paces with increased head movement, they can unseat. Ear hooks or wing tips (Beats Fit Pro's flexible wingtip, Anker Sport X10's ear hook) lock into the anti-helix and stay secure at any pace. If you've had earbuds fall out while running, buy a hook or wingtip design.

Sweat Resistance Ratings Explained

IP55 means the earbuds resist water jets and dust — more than adequate for sweat and rain. IP68 means full submersion resistance, which is overkill for running. IPX4 (splash resistant only) is the minimum acceptable for running earbuds. Verify the IP rating in the spec sheet, not just the 'sweat resistant' marketing copy.

Open-Ear vs. Sealed Earbuds for Safety

Some runners prefer not to seal their ear canal so they can hear traffic. Open-ear designs (bone conduction: Shokz OpenRun) leave ears completely open. Ambient sound modes on sealed earbuds (Jabra, Beats) let in traffic noise without removing the earbuds. For road running near traffic, some form of ambient awareness is strongly recommended.
