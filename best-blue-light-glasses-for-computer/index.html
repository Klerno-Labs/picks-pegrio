Blue-light glasses for computer work: check the evidence first

Sources checked October 10, 2026 · By Picks by Pegrio

Evidence note — October 10, 2026

This guide corrects unsupported health and product claims. Sources are linked where used. It is not a hands-on test or individualized health advice. This revision used AI-assisted research; there are no affiliate purchase links on this page.

We cannot support the earlier promise that blue-light glasses reliably improve sleep or reduce screen glare. If those benefits are the reason you are shopping, read the evidence before paying for a lens upgrade.

What the review found

The 2023 Cochrane review covered 17 randomized trials with 619 participants. It found that filtering lenses may offer no short-term advantage for computer-related eye strain; effects on sleep were uncertain. The review searched studies through March 2022, so it does not establish the performance of every current product.

Separate the purchasing questions

Ask an optician what the quoted price includes: prescription, frame, anti-reflective coating, tint and any blue-light filter are separate features. A product’s blue-light percentage does not, by itself, establish a health benefit. We removed the earlier unsupported percentages assigned to whole tint categories.

Check fit, lens specifications, prescription suitability, return conditions and whether the upgrade adds a benefit that matters to you. If symptoms are the concern, an eye-care professional can help identify the cause rather than assuming a filter is the answer.

Correction record

October 10, 2026: removed claims of reliable sleep improvement, generalized filtering percentages, an incorrect review date, and unverified product rankings and prices. No hands-on eyewear test is claimed. The previous merchant recommendations have been withdrawn until their exact products and claims are reviewed.
