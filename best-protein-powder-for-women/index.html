Protein powder: label and suitability checks before buying

Sources checked October 10, 2026 · By Picks by Pegrio

Evidence note — October 10, 2026

This guide corrects unsupported health and product claims. Sources are linked where used. It is not a hands-on test or individualized health advice. This revision used AI-assisted research; there are no affiliate purchase links on this page.

A label aimed at women does not establish your protein needs or make a powder suitable for you. The earlier article gave one daily target to every reader and made blanket assurances about lactose tolerance. We have removed both.

Work out whether a supplement serves a need

The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements consumer guide explains that performance supplements do not replace a nutritionally adequate diet and recommends discussing them with a health-care provider, particularly when medical conditions or medications are relevant. A shopping guide cannot set an individualized intake target.

Compare the exact label

Look at the protein amount per serving, serving size, servings per container, ingredient and allergen statements, and delivered cost. Flavors and versions can have different formulas. Do not assume a blend is lactose-free because one of its ingredients is whey isolate, or infer tolerance from a brand name.

Our former rule that a powder below a particular protein percentage contains “excessive filler” was not a defensible quality standard. Compare what the product contains with the purpose it would serve in your diet. If you have an allergy, dietary restriction or medical condition, confirm suitability with the appropriate professional and exact product label.
