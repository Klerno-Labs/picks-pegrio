Travel power banks: check airline rules and charging compatibility

Sources checked October 10, 2026 · By Picks by Pegrio

Evidence note — October 10, 2026

This guide corrects unsupported product and safety claims. Sources are linked where used. It is not a hands-on test or a personal suitability assessment. This revision used AI-assisted research; clearly marked merchant links may earn us an affiliate commission.

Choose a travel power bank by checking two things separately: permission to carry it on your itinerary and compatibility with the devices you want to charge. U.S. battery rules include an airline-approval exception above 100 Wh; other carrier and international limits can differ.

Start with the marked watt-hour rating

The FAA’s current PackSafe guidance generally permits lithium-ion batteries up to 100 Wh. It describes airline approval for up to two larger spare batteries in the 101–160 Wh range. Airlines can impose stricter capacity and quantity limits, so confirm with each carrier before travel.

Power banks belong in the cabin, not checked baggage. Remove them if your carry-on is checked at the gate, and protect them from damage and short circuits. Check current recall information for the exact model; do not bring a damaged or recalled battery. Read the FAA guidance and your airline’s policy for full details.

Match output to the device

Compare the bank’s per-port output profiles with the input specifications of your phone or laptop and the cable you will use. A USB-C socket or a large advertised wattage does not guarantee the charging speed your device accepts. Check how output changes when several ports are in use and how you will recharge the bank itself.

Use the battery’s marked Wh rating for the travel check. Capacity in mAh alone omits the voltage needed for a conversion. Nor does the advertised battery capacity guarantee a particular number of complete device charges; we have not measured conversion losses or runtime.
