A fitness tracker is most useful for the metrics you'll actually check daily. For most people, that's steps, sleep score, and resting heart rate trend. The expensive features — ECG, SpO2, built-in GPS, stress scoring — go unchecked within 3 months for the majority of users. What to Look for in a Fitness Tracker Under $100

Step Counting Accuracy

Sleep Tracking: What Actually Matters

Sleep stage tracking (light/deep/REM) is less accurate than total sleep duration on all wrist-worn trackers. The useful insight is trend consistency: did your sleep score trend down this week? That's more actionable than knowing you got exactly 47 minutes of REM. Fitbit's sleep algorithm is the most validated in the industry. All trackers in this guide provide sleep duration tracking adequate for trend monitoring.

Heart Rate Monitoring Accuracy

Wrist-based heart rate sensors are accurate within ±2-5 bpm during rest and steady-state cardio (walking, jogging, cycling at consistent pace). During HIIT, weightlifting, or interval training, accuracy drops to ±10-20 bpm due to lag in blood flow detection. For zone training (target heart rate zones), a chest strap (Polar H10, $89) remains significantly more accurate. For general fitness tracking and trend monitoring, wrist-based HR is sufficient.

Battery Life and Charging

The Xiaomi Band 8 runs 16 days per charge. Fitbit Inspire 3 runs 10 days. Garmin Vivosmart 5 runs 7 days. Amazfit Band 7 runs 18 days. Every extra feature (SpO2 overnight, continuous HR at 1-second intervals, always-on display) cuts battery life. Sleep tracking is the use case where battery matters most — if your tracker dies overnight, you lose the most valuable data window. All picks in this guide last at least 5 days, ensuring reliable sleep tracking.

App Ecosystem and Data Portability

The app experience determines how useful your tracker data becomes. Fitbit's app is the most user-friendly with clear visualizations and actionable insights. Garmin Connect is the most data-rich for athletes. Xiaomi Health and Zepp (Amazfit) are functional but basic. Consider whether the app integrates with Apple Health or Google Fit (all picks in this guide do). Data portability matters if you switch trackers — Fitbit and Garmin both allow data export.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are fitness trackers under $100 accurate?

Step counting varies by ±10-15% across all trackers, including $300+ models. The Fitbit Inspire 3 is consistently most accurate under $100. Heart rate during rest and steady-state cardio is within ±5 bpm of chest straps. During HIIT, wrist accuracy drops to ±15-20 bpm. For daily activity tracking and trend monitoring, sub-$100 trackers are sufficiently accurate.

Which is better: Fitbit or Garmin under $100?

Fitbit (Inspire 3) excels at daily activity, sleep tracking, and user-friendly app. Garmin (Vivosmart 5) excels at running metrics and training analysis. If you walk and track steps — Fitbit. If you run and do structured training — Garmin. Both are $99.

Do I need GPS in a fitness tracker?

Only for outdoor running, biking, or hiking where you want route mapping and pace data. Connected GPS (via phone) provides this but requires your phone nearby. For indoor workouts and general step tracking, GPS is unnecessary. No sub-$100 tracker has built-in GPS — all use connected GPS.

Can a fitness tracker under $100 measure blood oxygen?

Yes — SpO2 sensors are now common under $100. The Fitbit Inspire 3, Garmin Vivosmart 5, and Xiaomi Band 8 all include SpO2. However, wrist-based SpO2 is less accurate than fingertip oximeters (±2-3% vs ±1%). SpO2 is most useful for overnight trend monitoring, not real-time medical readings.

How long do fitness tracker batteries last?

Battery ranges from 5-18 days. Xiaomi Band 8: 16 days. Fitbit Inspire 3: 10 days. Garmin Vivosmart 5: 7 days. Amazfit Band 7: 18 days. Features that drain battery: continuous HR, SpO2 overnight, always-on display, connected GPS. Disabling these extends battery by 30-50%.
