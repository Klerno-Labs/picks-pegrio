Smart-home starter guide: one room, compatible devices

Sources checked October 10, 2026 · By Picks by Pegrio

For a first smart-home purchase, choose one useful task: switch a lamp on a schedule, dim the lights, or control a room from an app. Then identify the devices needed for that task. A bundle is only good value if its exact products work with the system you already have.

The Tapo US specification documents a Wi-Fi plug with Matter support, not a Thread plug. Its US electrical limits and hardware version matter; do not apply a differently named regional plug’s specifications. The manufacturer’s manual (PDF) requires a matching Matter controller and ecosystem app for that setup route. This is a single-device example, not a speaker-and-plug bundle we have priced or tested.

The exact Hue kit includes the four color-capable bulbs and Bridge Pro. Its listed bulb communications are Bluetooth/Zigbee, with Matter provided through a Hue Bridge. The bridge is part of the system, not an optional item to silently omit from a price comparison. Check the current listing for its full contents and your phone’s app requirements; other Hue starter kits differ.

Matter is not the same thing as Wi-Fi or Thread

The Connectivity Standards Alliance’s Matter FAQ describes Matter operating over Wi-Fi, Ethernet and Thread. A bridge can connect supported devices using other protocols. That does not make an arbitrary Zigbee device a native Matter device, or promise that every vendor feature is available in every app.

For a concrete example, Google’s current hub requirements list Nest Mini as a Matter-over-Wi-Fi hub, while Nest Hub (2nd gen) also supports Thread. A speaker’s brand name alone is not enough to choose a controller. The same Google guidance notes that supported device categories arrive in different platforms at different times.

Build a small shopping list

  1. Choose the control app. Start with what household members will actually use. Confirm each exact model’s supported features in that app.
  2. Inventory existing equipment. Note your router, any hub or bridge, phone requirements and the location of the first device. Check the manufacturer’s current setup requirements before paying for more hardware.
  3. Price the complete task. Count the controller, bridge, bulbs or plug, plus any optional paid features you intend to use. Compare the same quantities and versions.
  4. Keep a manual fallback. Consider how someone will operate the lamp or room controls if a phone is unavailable. Choose return terms that let you assess the setup in your home.

Use a plug only with equipment permitted by both manufacturers’ instructions and load limits. A Matter logo does not establish electrical suitability or guaranteed operation. If the next project involves heating or cooling, use our thermostat compatibility guide before buying.
