Home-office monitors: verify the signal, power and stand separately

Choose a monitor around the computer and desk you already have. Confirm the display signal, resolution, power requirements and physical fit before comparing extra USB ports. A shared connector shape does not mean every port performs the same job.

Read the exact model suffix

The Dell UltraSharp U2722D specification lists a 27-inch QHD display, HDMI and DisplayPort inputs, USB-C upstream and a USB-C downstream port rated up to 15 W. Its stand adjusts height, tilt, swivel and pivot; the VESA interface is 100 × 100 mm.

That downstream 15 W specification is not evidence of high-power laptop charging or a single-cable video connection. The shared U2722D/U2722DX/U2722DE manual distinguishes the models. Match the correct suffix and port diagram before choosing a cable or dock.

Plan the connection in full

Identify your computer’s supported video outputs, desired resolution and refresh rate. Then check the monitor input and cable together. If you want charging through the display, confirm the exact Power Delivery rating against the laptop’s needs; the mere presence of USB-C is insufficient.

Check how any USB hub operates, whether a separate upstream connection is needed and which peripherals you will attach. We have not tested docking, daisy chaining or device compatibility.

Check working space and adjustment

Measure desk depth, stand footprint and the position you need. If using an arm, confirm the mount pattern and load against the monitor without its stand. Preview text scaling in your operating system when possible; resolution alone does not determine comfortable text size.

Compare current availability, the selected seller’s warranty and return rules, including pixel policies. This guide does not establish present stock of the older U2722D or rank every current monitor.
