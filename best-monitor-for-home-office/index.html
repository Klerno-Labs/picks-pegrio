The right home office monitor depends on what you do. For spreadsheets and documents, 1440p at 27 inches is the sweet spot — text is crisp without needing display scaling. For design and photo work, 4K with accurate color (Delta E < 2) is worth the premium. For video calls and general browsing, 1080p at 24 inches is entirely sufficient.

Panel Type: IPS vs. VA vs. TN

IPS panels have the best color accuracy and widest viewing angles — essential for color-critical work and comfortable for all-day use. VA panels have higher contrast ratios, making dark content look better, but have slower response times. TN panels are fastest but poorest for color and viewing angles. Home office use: buy IPS.

Resolution and Pixel Density

1080p on a 27-inch monitor looks noticeably soft to most people because pixel density drops to 81 PPI. 1440p at 27 inches is 108 PPI — text and icons are visibly sharper. 4K at 27 inches is 163 PPI, which requires display scaling at 125-150% on Windows to avoid microscopic UI elements.

USB-C Single-Cable Workflow

Monitors with USB-C inputs that deliver video signal + power + USB hub allow a single-cable docking setup — plug one cable into your laptop and you have monitor, keyboard, mouse, and charging. The LG 27UK850-W and Dell U2722D both support USB-C single-cable docking. This is the most underrated quality-of-life upgrade for laptop users.
