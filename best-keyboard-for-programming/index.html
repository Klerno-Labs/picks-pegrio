Switch Type for Programming

Brown switches (tactile, quiet) are the most popular for programmers — you feel the actuation point without the loud click of blues. Blue switches sound satisfying but become annoying on video calls. Red switches (linear, light) cause more errors for touch typists because there's no tactile feedback. Most programmers settle on brown or tactile clears.

Tenkeyless vs. Full Size

Tenkeyless (TKL) keyboards drop the numpad, which moves your mouse closer to center and reduces shoulder strain. For programming, the numpad is rarely used. The Keychron K2 is a 75% layout — even more compact, retaining function row and arrow keys. Most programmers prefer TKL or 75% for ergonomic reasons.

Wireless for Clean Desk Setups

The Keychron K2 is wireless (Bluetooth 5.1) with a 4000mAh battery lasting 9-15 days per charge. The Das Keyboard 4 is wired-only. For a clean desk without cable management, wireless is worth the slight latency trade-off (imperceptible for typing, not recommended for competitive gaming).
