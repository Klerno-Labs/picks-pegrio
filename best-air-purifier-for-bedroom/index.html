How we research and correct articles.

Bedroom air purifiers face a unique challenge that living-room and office purifiers don't: they need to filter significant volumes of air while remaining quiet enough to sleep through. A noisy purifier at 2am defeats the purpose — you'll turn it off, and your bedroom air goes unfiltered for the 8 hours when you're breathing deepest and longest. CADR (Clean Air Delivery Rate) tells you how fast a unit cleans; the sleep mode noise rating tells you whether you'll actually use it overnight.

What to Look for in a Bedroom Air Purifier

CADR Ratings: Match It to Your Room Size

CADR (Clean Air Delivery Rate) measures how much filtered air the unit delivers per minute, measured separately for smoke, dust, and pollen. The rule of thumb: multiply your room's square footage by 1.5 to get the minimum CADR. A 150 sq ft bedroom needs CADR 225+. A 250 sq ft bedroom needs CADR 375+. All picks in this guide publish their CADR — avoid brands that don't, as they're typically hiding low performance.

Sleep Mode Noise: Below 30 dB

True HEPA vs. HEPA-Type

True HEPA is a regulated standard: 99.97% of particles at 0.3 microns. "HEPA-type" and "HEPA-style" are marketing terms with no standard — they typically capture 90-95% at 2+ microns, missing the finest and most harmful particles (fine dust, smoke, some bacteria). All picks in this guide use True HEPA or equivalent certification. Avoid any bedroom purifier that doesn't specify True HEPA.

Annual Filter Replacement Costs

Filter replacement is the hidden cost of air purification. True HEPA filters need replacement every 6-12 months ($20-50 each). Carbon pre-filters need replacement every 3-6 months ($10-25 each). Annual costs range from $30 (Levoit Core 300) to $80+ (Winix with AOC carbon). Factor this into your purchase decision — a $89 purifier that costs $80/year to maintain is more expensive over 3 years than a $149 purifier at $40/year.

Air Changes Per Hour (ACH)

For allergy sufferers, the unit should filter the room's entire air volume 4+ times per hour (4 ACH). Calculate: room sq ft × ceiling height × 4 / 60 = required CFM. A 150 sq ft bedroom with 8-foot ceilings needs 80 CFM. The Levoit Core 300 delivers 135 CFM — well above the threshold. All picks in this guide achieve 4+ ACH in their rated room sizes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where should I place an air purifier in my bedroom?

Place it 3-5 feet from your bed, ideally upwind of the door. Don't place it against a wall — air purifiers need 12+ inches of clearance on all sides. Avoid corners. If you have HVAC, place the purifier near the return vent. Never place it directly on your nightstand — proximity creates noise and draft issues.

How long should I run my bedroom air purifier?

Run it 24/7 on Auto or Sleep mode. Air purifiers are designed for continuous operation and use minimal electricity ($15-40/year). If noise is a concern, use Sleep mode overnight and Turbo when you're out. Running for only a few hours leaves the air unfiltered for most of the day.

Do air purifiers help with allergies?

Yes, significantly. True HEPA filters capture 99.97% of particles at 0.3 microns, including pollen, dust mite debris, pet dander, and mold spores. Studies show bedroom HEPA purifiers reduce allergic rhinitis symptoms by 30-50% in controlled trials. The key is running it continuously and replacing filters on schedule.

How often do I need to replace HEPA filters?

Every 6-12 months for True HEPA, depending on air quality and usage. Pre-filters should be cleaned every 2-3 months. Carbon filters for odor last 3-6 months. A clogged HEPA reduces CADR by 40-60% and increases motor strain. Annual costs range from $20 (Levoit Core 300) to $80 (Winix with AOC carbon).
